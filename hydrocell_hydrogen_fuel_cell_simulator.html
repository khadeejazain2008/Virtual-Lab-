3. How the fuel cell works

Anode: oxidationCathode: reductionOverall: redox reaction

This is a simplified proton exchange membrane (PEM) fuel cell. Hydrogen is oxidized at the anode. Protons cross the membrane, while electrons travel through an external circuit and can power a load. At the cathode, oxygen combines with protons and electrons to form water.

4. Electrode reactions

Anode – oxidation

2H₂ → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻

Cathode – reduction

O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂O

Overall cell reaction

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O + electrical energy + heat

The electrons released at the anode travel through the external circuit. The membrane allows protons to pass but keeps the gases separated.

5. Working principle – four steps

1. Hydrogen enters

Hydrogen gas reaches the anode catalyst, where oxidation takes place.

2. Protons form

Hydrogen separates into protons and electrons at the anode.

3. Electrons flow

Electrons travel through an external circuit and deliver electrical energy to a load.

4. Water forms

At the cathode, oxygen reacts with protons and electrons to form water.

6. Calculation method

Overall ratio: 2 mol H₂ : 1 mol O₂ : 2 mol H₂O
Electrons transferred: n(e⁻) = 2 × n(H₂ reacted)
Charge: Q = n(e⁻) × F, where F ≈ 96,485 C mol⁻¹
Electrical energy estimate: E = Q × V

The simulator uses the stoichiometric ratio to identify the limiting reactant. It then estimates charge and electrical energy using the selected operating voltage. It does not model real-world losses, gas pressure, temperature, humidity, or detailed reaction kinetics.
